Rules

Rules at a glance
  1. Reach the edge opposite your start to win. On your turn, move one square or spend a wall.
  2. A wall spans two squares and sits in a groove. It may not overlap or cross another wall.
  3. No wall may seal any player off completely. Everyone keeps a path to their goal.
  4. Adjacent to an opponent you may jump straight over it. If that square is blocked, jump diagonally to either side instead.
  5. Two player games give ten walls each. Four player games give five. First to the far edge wins.
  6. Walls are painted in the colour of whoever placed them, so you can read each side's blocking plan at a glance.

Three things to know before your first wall

01

Set the seats

Pick two or four players, then set each seat to human, AI, or off. One human against three AIs is a legal table.

02

Move or block

Click a glowing cell to step, or click a groove to drop a wall. The preview turns red when a wall would seal somebody in.

03

Read the race

Each card shows walls left, distance to goal, and a win rate sampled from 160 simulated finishes of the position you are in.

Questions

Is this the official Quoridor?

No. Quoridor was designed by Mirko Marchesi and published by Gigamic in 1997. This is a third party reimplementation of the rules for study and play, with no affiliation, endorsement, or sponsorship from the publisher. The name and the game belong to their owners.

Do I need an account, a download, or an internet connection?

None of the three. The page is plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript with no build step and no third party requests, so you can open the file from disk and play on a plane. Opened that way the Web Workers are blocked, and the engine quietly computes on the main thread instead.

How strong is the AI?

It searches with iterative deepening alpha beta and a Zobrist hashed transposition table, for about 2.4 seconds per move. It sees straight and diagonal jumps, and it only considers walls that actually block someone's shortest path. That is strong enough to punish loose play, and honest enough to say it is not a titled engine.

What do the win rate bars actually measure?

A Monte Carlo estimate. The engine plays out the current position many times with a fast greedy policy and counts who finishes first. One batch credits every player from the same sample set, so the bars stay consistent with each other and add up to about one hundred percent.

Can I play three players, or one against three AIs?

Yes. Seats set to off are dropped from the game, so any mix that leaves at least two active seats is valid. Four player games give each seat five walls instead of ten, which is what keeps a crowded board playable.

Can I play with the keyboard?

Tab to the board, then use the arrow keys to move a cursor and Enter to play. Press W to switch into wall placement, arrow keys to pick a groove, R to rotate the wall, and Escape to return to moving. Every move is also announced in the status line.

What does undo do in a game against the AI?

It rewinds the AI replies along with your own last action, so you land back on your own turn with the position you meant to change. It stops at your previous decision rather than making you press the button once per seat.

Where is my data going?

Nowhere. The game collects nothing and sends nothing. Your theme choice and two display toggles are kept in this browser's local storage so they survive a reload, and that is the whole of it.
